(For one two-crust pie)
2 c. flour
1 tsp. salt
3, c. shortening

For best results, all ingredients
should be well chilled.
Sift the flour and salt together into a
bowl. Cut the shortening into the
flour with a pastry blender, knives or
your fingers only until the mixture
resembles very coarse crumbs. It’s
OK if some of the pieces of shorten-
ing are the size of small peas. (See
note, above, about overworking pie
dough.)
Sprinkle in the water, a littie at a
time, stirring lightly with a fork and
incorporating all the particles stick-
ing to the sides of the bowl. Stop
adding water when the dough heslds
together in a ball without any dry
spots. It should not be wet.
General instructions: Divide the
dough in two, and, working on a
lightly floured surface, roll each half
out until it is bigger by about an inch
‘all around than the pie pan. (I begin
by flouring top and bottom of the
dough and pushing it out with my
hands until it’s fairly flat before I
start using the rolling pin.)
Trim the crusts into circles. Roll the
bottom crust onto the rolling pin,
unroll it over the pan and allow it to
subside into the pan without forcing,
gently adjusting fit where necessary,
Fill, cover with top crust, crimp edge
with fork or fingers, slit top crust
and bake according to instructions
for the filling.
Place strips or a ring of aluminum
foil on top of the edge of the crust
for the first 15 minutes of baking, to
prevent the edge from burning. -
