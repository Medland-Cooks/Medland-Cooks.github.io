Now something from my mom. I've
printed this recipe in the paper be-
fore, but it was a very long time ago,
and it wasn’t as appropriate for that
article as it is for this one. I've al-
ways had a special place in my heart
for these bar cookies because they
were a relatively rare treat: My
mom almost never made them for
us; they were always for company.
(But I ate a lot of them when they
were served to the company, so I
guess I did all right.)
Many very different recipes exist
with this same name. They make
many very different things.

4 eggs
2 ¢. brown sugar
1 tsp. cinnamon
Dash cloves
Dash nutmeg
2 c. flour
11, to 2 c. raisins
1, ¢. (or more, why not?) semisweet
chocolate chips ,
Icing: '
1 c. confectioners’ sugar, sifted
2 tbsp. milk
Preheat the oven to 350 degrees.
Beat the eggs. Beat in brown sugar,
cinnamon, cloves and nutmeg. Add
the flour, mixing well, then stir in
the raisins and chocolate chips.
Pour into a greased 9- by 13-inch
pan. Bake 35 minutes or so, until a
toothpick inserted in the center
emerges clean or with a nice crumb
or two. Put on racks to cool. ‘
Meanwhile, combine the sifted con-
fectioners’ sugar with the milk to
make a thin icing and spread it over
the still-warm bars. If you want them
like my mom’s, cool before cutting.
My mom always cut them into paral-
lelograms (but she never called
them that): Cut first into 1-inch-wide
strips along the length of the pan.
Make the second sét of cuts diagonal-
ly, about 2 inches apart. There you
go — parallelograms.
The icing always dries hard and brit-
tle, so when you wait to cut them, as
~my mom always did, you get shards
and fragments of icing on each one. I
always liked that about them, but I
don’t know why. : ,
 Store in an airtight container.
